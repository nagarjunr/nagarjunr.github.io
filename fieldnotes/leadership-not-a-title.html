Leadership is a relationship, not a title.

By Nagarjun Rajendran / 7 October 2026

For a long time I believed I needed the right title before I could influence anyone.

That belief has been changing over the last two years, mostly through self-discovery and conversations with mentors and coaches.

Nowadays I share what I learn about tech and soft skills, and anyone can pick what is useful and ignore the rest. Nobody assigned me that. A recent course at Gies College of Business, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, Leading Teams, made this clearer to me.

I turned the first half of the course, Developing as a Leader, into a 10-slide carousel. It is for anyone moving from doing the work to leading it. It covers leadership as a relationship and not a title, self-awareness, biases like anchoring and availability, emotional intelligence and trust, negotiating on interests instead of positions, and the three Cs (curiosity, courage, commitment) along with six traits of inclusive leaders.

Download the carousel (PDF)

Which of these do you wish you had learned earlier? I would love to hear.

The course is Leading Teams: Developing as a Leader on Coursera. Thanks to Elizabeth A. Luckman, Kari Keating and Denise Lewin Loyd for teaching it. The videos, readings and weekly reflections let me test the ideas against my own work.
