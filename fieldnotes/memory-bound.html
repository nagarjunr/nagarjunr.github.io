Most people buy GPUs for TFLOPs. That is the wrong number to optimize.

By Nagarjun Rajendran / 27 July 2026

I just published the deep dive of Silicon to Systems: The Anatomy of Scale, and it starts with an uncomfortable truth about LLM inference: your GPU’s tensor cores spend most of their time starved, not computing.

Here is why.

  • The energy tax. Fetching a weight from HBM costs about 35x more energy than the math operation itself. Compute is cheap. Moving data is where the power goes.
  • The roofline reality. At batch=1 decode, an H100 operates at about 1 FLOP/byte, roughly 295x below its actual ceiling. You are not compute bound. You are memory bound, every single token.
  • The capacity wall. A single Llama-3 70B request at 128K context needs about 40 GB just for the KV cache, before you have loaded a single model weight.

This is the physics that governs your inference cost, your latency SLAs, and why "faster" hardware does not always mean faster tokens.

In this issue I break down the Energy Tax, the Roofline Model, the KV Cache math, the HBM4 roadmap, and the three software techniques (quantization, FlashAttention, speculative decoding) that actually move the needle.
