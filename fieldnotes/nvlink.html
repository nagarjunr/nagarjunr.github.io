Eight GPUs were never eight times the machine.

By Nagarjun Rajendran / 17 August 2026

Buy eight GPUs, get eight times the compute. That math only works if they never have to talk to each other.

I just published the deep dive of Silicon to Systems: The Anatomy of Scale, and it goes one level below the memory wall I wrote about earlier: the physics of what happens between GPUs, not inside them.

Here is why it matters.

  • The naive assumption breaks immediately. Training is not embarrassingly parallel. Every step requires gradient sync, activation exchange, or both. The moment GPUs communicate, your speedup is capped by interconnect bandwidth, not teraflops.
  • PCIe was never built for this. Even its best case peer-to-peer transfers hit only 75 to 90 percent of an already modest theoretical bandwidth, and that number drops further the moment multiple GPUs contend for the same bus.
  • NVLink closes the gap by an order of magnitude. From 160 GB/s at its first generation to 1.8 TB/s per GPU today, roughly 14 times a PCIe Gen5 link. NVSwitch turns that into a fabric where every GPU reaches every other GPU at full bandwidth, no CPU in the path.
  • The cliff still exists. Cross a rack boundary onto InfiniBand and bandwidth drops roughly 18x. That single number decides which parallelism strategy your model can even use.
Architect's take: eight GPUs were never eight times the machine. They are one machine, if the fabric between them lets them act like one.
