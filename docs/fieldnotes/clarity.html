Clarity is part of the architecture.

By Nagarjun Rajendran / 22 November 2026

A diagram can show every component and still leave a team unsure who makes the next decision.

That uncertainty becomes visible at the boundaries. The platform team assumes the application team owns an alert. The application team assumes the device team validates the data. Each local assumption sounds reasonable. The whole system has a gap.

Describe the decision, not just the component.

When discussing an interface, I find it useful to ask who owns its meaning. Who decides whether a message is valid? Who changes the schema? Who is responsible when a reading arrives but cannot be used?

A named service owner helps. A shared understanding of the decisions that owner can make helps more.

Leave a short record.

An architecture decision does not need a long document. A concise record can capture:

  • The problem and the constraints that matter.
  • The options considered and why one was chosen.
  • The tradeoff being accepted.
  • The owner and the condition that would trigger a review.

This gives the next person a reason they can inspect. It also gives the team permission to revisit a decision when its assumptions change.

Make room for others to act.

Technical leadership is not limited to a management role. It can mean reducing ambiguity so someone else can move forward with confidence.

That might be a clear handoff between teams, a smaller proof of concept, or an explicit boundary for a decision. The goal is to help people make progress without waiting for every detail to return to the same person.

Clarity is something a team can build and maintain.

For me, that is part of systems thinking. The people who build and operate a system are part of the architecture conversation.

Connected systems. Clear thinking.
